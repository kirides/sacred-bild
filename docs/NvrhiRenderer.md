# NVRHI renderer (plan)

Status: plan, stage 1 in progress. Branch `claude/nvrhi-renderer`. Nothing here changes the default: `[DDraw] Backend=d3d9`
stays what players get, `Backend=chain` stays the escape hatch, and the new renderer is `Backend=nvrhi`, opt-in until it is
at least as good as the Direct3D 9 backend in looks and frame times.

## Goal

A renderer of SacredBild's own, on [NVRHI](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/NVRHI) (vendored at
`648f745142a929a1a61c91799a3be1f6969bbcf8`), that takes over Sacred's rendering: Bild owns the device, the swap chain and the
frame, and draws the world (ground, water, objects, models, shadows, effects, weather) and the interface from the game's own
state instead of replaying its Direct3D 7 calls. The game keeps simulating, culling and building its draw lists; Bild reads
them and draws them its way (few large draws, data kept on the GPU, any resolution, post-processing).

What the references do (facts only, no code taken; both are full remakes that read the game's files, not hooks):

- [SacredEngineRemake](https://github.com/Aytackydln/SacredEngineRemake) (C#, D3D12): terrain baked per sector, sprites as
  structured-buffer instances drawn in the native painter order, the five render queues and their depth rules
  (`docs/research/world-object-depth.md`, `world-material-blend-depth.md`, `texture-pak/`). The ground-on-the-GPU idea
  behind `[Render] GroundMesh` already came from there.
- [OpenSacred](https://github.com/zarcsis/OpenSacred) (Rust, Bevy/wgpu): the most complete written RE of the world pass:
  `docs/re/drawing.md` (row walk 0x62AE90, the 24-byte draw-list entries and their kinds, liquid cells, footprints, models and
  their shadows), `liquids.md`, `daynight.md`, `weather.md`, `effects.md`, and `docs/05-renderer-vulkan-4k.md` (projection,
  ~17 render states, 2 texture stages, fonts through GDI, ~9,600 draws per frame zoomed out).

Every address taken from them is checked against the decomp (`B:\DevDrive\sources\decomp-sacred`, `src_extra/` for
vtable-only functions such as cWorldView0::render 0x632140) and the Ghidra GOG program before it goes into
`sacred_addr.h`, with its signature generated by `tools/sigs/gen_sigs.py`.

## Which NVRHI backend

Checked 2026-11-27 by building the pinned commit for Win32 with VS 2026 (MSVC 14.51):

| Backend | x86 | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| D3D11 | gone | removed upstream before this commit (`NVRHI_WITH_DX11=ON` is a CMake error) |
| D3D12 | builds after 8 small fixes | `SIZE_T` is `unsigned long` and `size_t` `unsigned int` on x86 (3 `getBytecode` calls), a few `uint64_t`/`size_t` mixes in `std::min`/`align`/one upload offset, two raytracing `static_assert`s that compare a 4-byte pointer with the 8-byte GPU address in the same union, a `>> 32` on a 32-bit `size_t`. Remaining warnings are `uint64_t` sizes narrowed to `size_t` for mapped ranges, which never exceed 4 GB here |
| Vulkan | builds after 6 small fixes | non-dispatchable handles are `uint64_t` on x86 and Vulkan-Hpp drops its handle conversions there: `VULKAN_HPP_TYPESAFE_CONVERSION=1` brings them back, and NVRHI's debug-name helper, two `nullptr` defaults and one log cast assumed pointer handles |

Both backends are built and selectable: `[Render] Api=d3d12|vulkan` under `Backend=nvrhi` (default d3d12). 32-bit d3d12.dll /
dxgi.dll ship with Windows 10 and 11 (SysWOW64); the 32-bit Vulkan loader and drivers are what DXVK uses. Wine/Proton: Vulkan
natively, D3D12 through vkd3d-proton. Shaders are HLSL compiled offline with the Windows SDK's `dxc`, to DXIL (shader model 6.0)
and to SPIR-V (`-spirv`), generated into `.inc` files like the existing `fxc` ones (`tools/re/gen_shaders.py`), so nothing
compiles shaders at run time.

Consequences for players: `Backend=nvrhi` needs a D3D12 GPU (feature level 11_0) on Windows 10+, or a Vulkan 1.3 driver.
`d3d12.dll`, `dxgi.dll` and `vulkan-1.dll` are delay-loaded (Vulkan's entry points are taken from the loader at run time), so
ddraw.dll still loads on Windows 7 and the other backends never touch them. If the device can't be made, the log says why and
the game runs on the Direct3D 9 backend.

32-bit address space is the main risk: the GOG Sacred.exe is not large-address-aware (characteristics 0x10F), so the process
has 2 GB, and a D3D12 driver takes more of it than D3D9 (its user-mode driver, descriptor heaps, upload rings). Stage 2 measures
the address space the device costs; budgets (upload ring, staging, texture copies kept for Lock) are kept small and logged
(`[Debug] D3DStats`).

## Architecture

```
src/bild/rhi/        NVRHI device, swap chain, frame (command lists, upload ring, fences), pipeline/sampler caches,
                     shader blobs, debug messages -> SacredBild.log
src/bild/ddrawrhi/   DirectDraw 7 / Direct3D 7 on rhi: the compatibility floor (stage 3)
src/bild/scene/      the take-over renderers: world target and frame graph, ground, water, sprites, models, effects,
                     UI (stages 5-10)
third_party/nvrhi/, third_party/DirectX-Headers/   vendored, pinned, with the x86 fixes listed in third_party/README.md
```

The compatibility floor is what makes this incremental: the game has many small draw paths (main menu, character choice,
loading screens, movies, maps, the help screen, the save portrait read-back, GDI text on surfaces) that a take-over renderer
would have to replace all at once before the first frame showed. Instead, stage 3 makes every Direct3D 7 call land on the same
NVRHI device, so `Backend=nvrhi` is playable early; the take-over stages then switch whole passes from "the game's D3D7 calls
through the floor" to "Bild's renderer from the game's state", each behind its own `[Render]` switch so a pass can be compared
or turned back. What is left on the floor at the end (menus, movies, rarely drawn screens) costs nothing per frame in play.

Bild's existing work above the device keeps working on the floor without changes (batcher, atlas, UI canvas, ground quads,
sprite quads, async/parallel animation), and Gear's RmlUi replay keeps drawing through the D3D7 device it gets. They move to
native paths in the take-over stages.

## Stages

Each stage ends with a local commit (via `tools/agent-git.ps1`, no version bumps), a clean build, and, where the game is
involved, a run in a test copy of the game (not the play folder) at 1600x900 with scale-to-fit, compared with `Backend=d3d9`
by screenshots and frame times. Visual stages are not merged until John says "merge" in the project thread. SacredReborn.exe:
best effort; a take-over pass whose addresses are not found in ReBorn turns itself off there and leaves that pass to the floor.

1. **Vendor and build.** NVRHI, DirectX-Headers (`v1.717.0-preview`) and Vulkan-Headers (`v1.4.352`), the tags NVRHI's CMake
   pins, under `third_party/`, the x86 fixes applied and listed, `nvrhi` + `nvrhi_d3d12` + `nvrhi_vk` built static into
   ddraw.dll (validation layer in), the system DLLs delay-loaded, a `dxc` step (DXIL and SPIR-V) in `gen_shaders.py`. No
   behaviour change.
2. **RHI core and `Backend=nvrhi`.** Adapter choice, device for either API (D3D12 debug layer / Vulkan validation layer with
   `[Debug] RhiValidation`), swap chain on the game window (DXGI flip model / VkSwapchainKHR) with a frame-latency waitable, resize, present, the frame limiter and VSync settings as today,
   NVRHI messages to the log, fallback to d3d9 on failure. Test: the window clears and presents; nothing else is drawn yet.
3. **Compatibility floor (DirectDraw 7 / Direct3D 7 on NVRHI).** The same subset the Direct3D 9 backend implements:
   - 3a surfaces and 2D: textures (the game's formats; ARGB4444 and friends expanded on upload where DXGI lacks them), system
     memory surfaces with GDI DCs, Lock/Unlock, Blt/BltFast, render target + z-buffer, read-back (screenshots, save portrait),
     the flip. Menus, loading screens, cursor and the interface show.
   - 3b the fixed-function device: one uber vertex/pixel shader pair with variants for the vertex formats the game uses
     (pretransformed, lit/unlit), 8 lights, material, 2 texture stages' combiners and alpha test as constants, blend/depth/
     cull as pipeline state (a cache keyed by state; the hot keys are few), dynamic vertex/index rings, state blocks. The world
     shows.
   - 3c SacredBild's own drawing: the ImGui overlay and controller prompts, Media Foundation movies, screenshots.
   Parity: the render-parity saves (`B:\DevDrive\sacred-shadowtest` recipe) against `Backend=d3d9`.
4. **Bild's GPU features on NVRHI.** GroundMesh, GpuSkinning, SMAA, the sharp texture filter as SM 6 shaders on the floor's
   device, so `Backend=nvrhi` matches d3d9 feature for feature. Frame times compared with d3d9 (idle and walking, zoomed in
   and out). From here `Backend=nvrhi` is a full alternative.
5. **The frame graph.** Bild owns the world's render targets: the world view is drawn into a color+depth target of its own
   size, post-processing runs on it, and the interface is composed over it at the window's resolution. Hooks: cWorldView0
   render (0x632140), cUI_Manager render (0x758ED0), the flip (0x645E40).
6. **Take-over: ground and water.** The ground from the sectors' tiles directly (GroundMesh's data becomes the source; the
   game's row walk keeps building the object lists but no longer draws a ground quad), the blend layers (0x62D3C0) composed in
   the ground shader, the liquid surfaces (0x62DD00; OpenSacred puts the liquid-cell table at the view's +0x3FF2C with its count at +0x80E3C, the decomp of 0x632140 resets +0x3FF1C and +0x80E3C: to settle) as a pass of their own.
7. **Take-over: statics and sprites.** The five draw lists (24-byte entries, filled by the row walk 0x62AE90) and the liquid
   cells' vector (0xAD7848) read after the game builds them and drawn as instanced quads in their order, with the depth rules
   the RE gives (depth only for the descriptor mask 0x200, the plane from screen Y and height), the player fade, footprints.
   The sprite pages shared as texture arrays/atlases; one or a few draws per list.
8. **Take-over: models and their shadows.** cObject3D drawModel (0x44A9D0) and the shadow call routed to Bild's skinned draw
   with the GPU skinning data (meshes and bone palettes already kept on the GPU), the flattened shadows drawn by Bild.
9. **Take-over: effects, weather, light.** The particle and model effects, the weather render (rain, fog layers, snow), the
   day/night light, glow passes (FX_RESSKIN).
10. **Take-over: the interface.** cUI_Manager's windows and fonts through a 2D batch renderer (glyph pages kept as textures
    instead of GDI surfaces where it pays), Gear's RmlUi through a native render interface (a Bild export in
    `src/core/bild_gear.h`; the D3D7 replay stays for the other backends), the overlay on the same 2D path.
11. **What is left.** Measure what still goes through the floor per frame in play (goal: nothing), ReBorn checks,
    docs (Rendering.md, Configuration.md), a frame-time report against d3d9.

## Design choices to confirm

- The compatibility floor (stage 3) as the base the take-over grows on, rather than a take-over-only renderer that shows
  nothing until every screen is replaced. Recommended; stages 1-2 are the same either way.
